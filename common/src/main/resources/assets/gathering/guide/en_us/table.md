# Sitting down to play

A table is three blocks by three and seats two, facing each other. Push tables together end to end, in a line, and they become one longer table: two seat four, three seat six, four seat eight, along the two long sides.

## Taking a seat

- Set a chair at the middle of a table's edge, facing the table, and right-click the chair to sit in it. That is the only way to take a seat.
- At a table on its own, the first player may sit at any edge. After that, only the edge opposite them seats anybody.
- A chair against any other part of a table watches. A watcher sees the game and plays no part in it.
- Sitting down at a free seat of a game already on asks first: **Join** it, or **Watch**. Right-click the table from that chair to be asked again.
- Crouch to get up, which gives up the seat. While a game is on you can also open the table's menu and choose **Leave table**.
- Getting up in the middle of a game, or leaving the server, keeps your seat for 8 minutes, and nobody else can sit there. Sit back down to carry on, or choose **Leave table** or concede to give it up. In a game of four or more, the other players can free the seat sooner if every one of them votes to, from the table's menu.

## Starting a game

1. Sit in a chair at the table. With no game on, the choice of game opens.
2. Choose the format and how many games, or free play, then press **Start**. Right-click the table from your chair to choose again.
3. The decks in your inventory are listed. Choose one and it goes down shuffled, or borrow one where the server lends them.

A deck that is not legal in the chosen format is still allowed. The screen shows what is not legal and asks whether to use it anyway. Use it and everybody at the table is told, and so is anybody who comes to it later.

Right-click the table while a game is on to sit at the board, and to choose a deck if yours is not down. Between games of a match, the same click lets you sideboard, or starts the next game.

## What the board tells you

The row along the top shows each player's face, life, hand and library, and whose turn it is. Beside the turn it says what the table is playing. Anything unusual, such as played for keeps or no format, is in amber. Rest the pointer there to read all of it.

Nothing here checks whether a play is legal. The people at the table do.
